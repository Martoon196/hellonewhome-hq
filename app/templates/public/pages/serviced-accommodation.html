Is it allowed?

The things we check before anyone lists a property

London: the 90-night rule

A whole home in Greater London can be let short-term for 90 nights a year without planning permission. Beyond that it needs consent, which boroughs rarely give. Our London offer runs the 90 nights of short stays, then switches to lets of 90 nights or more (contractors, relocations, students) or a tenancy. Shorter "mid-term" stays still count towards the 90.

Planning use class

Wales has a separate use class for short-term lets (C6), and councils can require planning permission through an Article 4 direction. England is consulting on the same. Your lease or mortgage may also forbid nightly letting. We check your address.

Business rates tests

Wales: available 252 nights and let 182 to be rated as a business rather than paying council tax (with a second-home premium if you miss). England: available 140 nights and let 70. We track it and tell you in good time if you're short.

Registers and levies

Wales: the visitor accommodation register opened on 1 October 2026; existing providers must register by 31 March 2027, new ones before their first guest, and councils can add a visitor levy from April 2027. England: a national short-let register is on the way. We handle registration and the returns either side of the border.

Safety and insurance, everywhere

Fire risk assessment, gas safety, EICR, PAT testing, and an insurance policy that actually covers paying guests. Many standard landlord policies don't. We'll put the rules that apply to your property in writing as part of the appraisal. This page is a summary, not legal advice.
